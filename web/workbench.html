The workbench

A bench, a shelf unit of two bays with a hook rail, two arms, and nineteen things. This is the world the demo happens in — a look at it, nothing more. Drag to turn the view, scroll to move in and out, and move the pointer over anything to put the spot on it.

Loading…

In this virtual world every object and every position is already known. No AI model works out what is on the bench or where it is. In a real room that half is genuinely hard, and that is what a vision-language model such as NVIDIA Cosmos is for.

We are building the other half: once the world is known, how does a person tell a robot which thing and which place?

What to look for

Three jars
In the bottom bay, left of centre. They are the same jar three times over: no word tells them apart, so only pointing can.
A fourth jar
Behind the middle one, in the back row. From this view you cannot see it, and neither can the robot. Orbit the camera up and it appears.
Two tubes of glue
To the right of the jars: one capped, one open. They differ by a state, not a word.
A bottle of oil
At the back right of the bench, 150 mm tall. It is taller than either bay is high, so asking for it to be put away has an honest answer: it cannot be.
The top board
Empty, and it stays empty. Reaching down onto anything much above 100 mm is not a pose this arm has, so the unit's lid is not a place it can use.

Every size here was measured

The geometry has one source, workbench/scene.py, and the physics scene is generated from it, so the shelf the arm reaches into and the shelf this page draws a spot on are the same shelf. The sizes rest on two measurements run against the real inverse kinematics: the widest thing the gripper holds through a lift and a move (76 mm on the jaws alone, 96 with the grip assist), and the reach ring at each height something here is gripped at. Reaching level into the bottom bay the arm cannot work closer in than 280 mm, which is why the shelf unit stands 50 mm further back than it was first drawn.

Nothing drives anything yet: no physics, no voice, no model. The arms are in their resting pose, and everything is exactly where the numbers put it.
